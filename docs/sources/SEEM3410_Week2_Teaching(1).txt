Agner Krarup Erlang and the origin of the Erlang formulas
Agner Krarup Erlang (1878–1929) was a Danish
mathematician, statistician, and engineer who
studied telephone traﬀic while working for the
Copenhagen Telephone Company.
• His practical question was: How many
circuits ( 電話線路 ) are needed when calls
arrive randomly and occupy circuits for
random lengths of time?
• He represented random arrivals , random call
durations , and limited circuits with
stochastic models.
Erlang’s lasting contribution is a modeling paradiagm that translates random
demand and service capacity into steady-state system performances .

Thus, a typical steady-state Poisson arrival sees state n with time-average probability p n .
High-level intuition:
• In a short interval of length d t , the probability of an arrival is λ d t + o (d t ) ,
independently of the current state .
• Thus, conditional on the system being in state N = n or N = n
′
, intervals of the same
length are equally likely to contain an arrival.
• Consequently, the fraction of arrivals that observe state n equals the fraction of time the
system spends in state n , namely p n .
PASTA lets us use the time-average probabilities p n to compute arrival-facing
(customer-facing) performance measures. It generally fails for non-Poisson arrivals.
Formal proof and precise conditions: R. W. Wolff (1982), “Poisson Arrivals See Time
Averages,” Operations Research 30(2), 223–231. doi:10.1287/opre.30.2.223 .

• For state n < c , arrivals occur at rate λ .
• In state n = c , an arrival is blocked and does not change the state.
• In state n ≤ c , departures occur at rate µ n = nµ .
